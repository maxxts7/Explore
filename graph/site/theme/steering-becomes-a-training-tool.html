Steering grows out of inference time

A steering vector was originally something applied after training finished, a late correction bolted onto a model whose weights were already fixed. This theme follows what happens when the same vector moves earlier, into the training run itself, so that a model is pushed away from an unwanted trait while it is still being shaped rather than afterward.

The page contrasts fixing a trait after finetuning against preventing it during finetuning, and follows the second approach through its refinements, applying the push across several layers at once, and its rivals, a system prompt carrying the same message and a direct penalty on activation change that turns out not to work. A closing case study shows the preventative version blocking an unwanted trait without blocking the genuinely new facts the training was meant to teach.

CAA-style steering, in the 2023 paper, was an inference-time patch applied to an already-finetuned model. Steering-based mitigation of finetuning-induced persona shifts is this paper's umbrella framing for moving the same lever earlier, into training itself, and it names two strategies. Post-hoc steering keeps steering where CAA left it -- subtracting a scaled persona vector at each decoding step after finetuning -- and works, though large coefficients cost general capability on MMLU. Preventative steering instead amplifies the persona vector during finetuning, so the model feels less pressure to encode the trait in its weights in the first place; it preserves capability better than post-hoc steering and, combined with multi-layer steering -- applying a layer-incremental vector at every layer instead of one -- suppresses trait acquisition to near-baseline without undoing the finetuning's intended learning. The paper checks preventative steering against alternatives at both ends of the prompting-training spectrum: preventative prompting, a system prompt eliciting the trait prepended to every training sample, performs comparably at a moderate coefficient; the train-time regularization baseline, penalizing activation change along the trait direction directly, proves largely ineffective, since the model routes around the penalized direction; and CAFT, a prior concept-ablation method, works for evil and sycophancy but not hallucination. The fact-acquisition case study is the sharpest test of the whole approach: finetuning on 1,000 postdating facts predictably increases hallucination, and preventative steering suppresses that increase while, unlike inference-time steering, still letting the model learn the new facts.

Members — a guided walk

  1. Steering-based mitigation of finetuning-induced persona shifts

    CAA-style steering, in the 2023 paper, was an inference-time patch applied to an already-finetuned model. Steering-based mitigation of finetuning-induced persona shifts is this paper's umbrella framing for moving that same lever earlier, into training itself, and it names two strategies -- one that keeps steering where CAA left it, and one that moves it into the finetuning process.

  2. Post-hoc (inference-time) steering mitigation

    Post-hoc steering is the first: at each decoding step after finetuning, a scaled persona vector is subtracted from the residual stream to suppress a trait the finetuning introduced. It works, but large coefficients cost general capability on MMLU -- a cost the paper's second strategy sets out to avoid.

  3. Preventative steering

    Preventative steering instead amplifies the persona vector during finetuning itself, relieving the model of pressure to encode the trait in its weights in the first place. It preserves capability better than post-hoc steering, and one further refinement pushes it closer still to suppressing trait acquisition entirely.

  4. Multi-layer steering with layer-incremental vectors

    Multi-layer steering is that refinement: instead of one vector at one layer, it applies a layer-incremental vector -- the difference between consecutive layers' persona directions -- at every layer, avoiding double-counting of overlapping directional information. Combined with preventative steering it suppresses trait acquisition to near-baseline without undoing the finetuning's intended learning. The paper then checks whether steering was even necessary, against three non-steering alternatives.

  5. Preventative prompting

    Preventative prompting is the first alternative, and the closest in spirit: a system prompt eliciting the undesired trait is prepended to every training sample instead of steering activations directly. At a moderate coefficient it performs comparably to preventative steering, though steering offers a wider control range -- a baseline that at least works, unlike the one that follows.

  6. Train-time projection-regularization baseline

    The train-time regularization baseline tries a more direct intervention: a loss term penalizing activation change along the trait direction during finetuning. It proves largely ineffective, since the model simply routes around the penalized direction and encodes the trait via alternative activation-space directions instead. A prior published method fares better, but only for some traits.

  7. CAFT (Concept Ablation Fine-Tuning)

    CAFT, Casademunt et al.'s 2025 Concept Ablation Fine-Tuning, zero-ablates a concept's activation-space projection during finetuning to prevent the model from learning correlations along it. Measured against preventative steering, it works for evil and sycophancy, whose base-model projections are already negative so ablation acts like positive preventative steering, but not for hallucination -- leaving preventative steering as the one method that held up across all three main traits, tested here on its sharpest case.

  8. Fact-acquisition case study

    The fact-acquisition case study is that sharpest test: finetuning on 1,000 facts postdating the training cutoff predictably increases hallucination, since new facts and fabricated ones are hard for the model to keep apart. Preventative steering suppresses that increase while, unlike inference-time steering, still letting the model learn the new facts -- the payoff for having moved the lever from inference time into training in the first place.
