Formal verification and control theory recur as safety building blocks

Across the paper's five problem areas, the same handful of prior formal-methods and control-theory tools keep reappearing as the technical substrate for proposed remedies. Formal verification, exemplified by the complete verification of the federal aircraft collision-avoidance system, stands as the paper's benchmark for what a rigorously safe system can look like, and motivates careful engineering as a reward-hacking remedy. Reachability analysis and robust policy improvement, both imported from control theory, recur specifically as tools for constructing conservative baseline policies against which impact regularizers and bounded exploration are defined. H-infinity control, a framework for minimizing worst-case response to bounded disturbance, underlies bounded exploration in the same way. Model repair extends the idea to a post-hoc setting, altering an already-trained model to restore a specified safety property. Asimov's first law rounds out the group as an early, informal attempt, later taken up seriously within classical AI planning by Weld and Etzioni, to make a safety property machine-checkable at all. This theme's claim is that formal guarantees are not one proposal among many but recurring infrastructure the paper draws on repeatedly.

Members — a guided walk

  1. Asimov's first law of robotics

    The earliest attempt in this group to make a safety property machine-checkable is fictional: Asimov's first law of robotics, which Weld and Etzioni tried to formalize within classical AI planning back in 1994. It stands as an aspiration without an engineering method behind it, a gap the next stop on this walk begins to close.

  2. Formal verification (of cyber-physical systems)

    Formal verification of cyber-physical systems supplies the rigor Asimov's law lacked (Formal verification (of cyber-physical systems) — supplies the rigor asimov lacked → Asimov's first law of robotics), exemplified by the complete formal verification of the federal aircraft collision-avoidance system decades later. It sets the paper's benchmark for what a rigorously safe system looks like, motivating careful engineering as a reward-hacking remedy.

  3. Reachability analysis

    Reachability analysis is a control-theoretic technique imported for a more modest but far more reusable purpose: determining which states a system can reach, or safely return to, from a given policy. Rather than verifying a whole system once, it recurs across the paper as the building block for constructing conservative baseline policies.

  4. H-infinity control

    H-infinity control reaches the same conservative envelope by a different route than reachability analysis (H-infinity control — reaches the same envelope by a different route than → Reachability analysis): rather than mapping reachable states directly, it minimizes worst-case response to bounded disturbances. The paper flags it as the framework underlying bounded exploration, a second use for the same worst-case logic.

  5. Robust policy improvement

    Robust policy improvement takes the safe set that reachability analysis supplies and adds a guarantee for leaving it, since reachability analysis supplies the safe set that bounds robust policy improvement (Reachability analysis — supplies the safe set that bounds → Robust policy improvement). Together the two are cited repeatedly as a matched pair for constructing safe baseline policies under model uncertainty.

  6. Model repair

    Model repair is the ex-post counterpart to reachability analysis (Model repair — is the ex post counterpart to → Reachability analysis): rather than constraining a policy ahead of time, it alters an already-trained model afterward to restore a specified safety property. It closes this walk by showing the same family of formal tools can intervene either before or after training.
