Projection as early warning: reading the persona before it speaks

A tool built to push a model's behavior in a chosen direction turns out to work just as well left alone: read the same direction against the model's current activations, without adding anything, and it predicts what the model is about to do, or what a training run has already done to it.

The page follows that one reading, a dot product between an activation and a trait direction, across three moments: taken at the end of a prompt, before any response, it forecasts what's about to be generated; taken across a finetuning run, it measures how far the model's persona has already shifted; taken against a training dataset before any training happens, it flags risk earliest of all.

Every quantity in this theme is the same operation -- a dot product between an activation and a persona vector, the residual-stream direction for a personality trait -- read at a different point in the model's life. Projection-based monitoring of prompt-induced persona shifts takes that dot product at the final token of a prompt, before the model has generated anything, and finds it correlates strongly (r=0.75-0.83) with the trait expression score of the response that follows. The paper validates this deployment-time monitor against two elicitation methods that push a model toward or away from a trait by construction: system-prompting, a custom instruction prepended to context, and many-shot prompting, which prepends dozens of trait-demonstrating exemplars instead of an instruction. The same object, read differently, becomes a training-time gauge. Finetuning shift projects the change in a model's average activation -- post-finetuning minus pre-finetuning -- onto the persona direction, and this shift predicts post-finetuning trait expression even more strongly (r=0.76-0.97) than the prompt-based projection predicts response-level expression. Projection difference pushes the same logic one step earlier still, comparing a training dataset's own response projections against the base model's natural projections on the same prompts to flag risk before any finetuning happens. Across all three settings, steering vectors are read rather than written: the same construction the corpus knows as a tool for injecting a behavior doubles here as a sensor for a shift the model has not yet, or has already, undergone.

Members — a guided walk

  1. Persona vector

    A persona vector is the residual-stream direction for a personality trait, the mean-difference activation pattern the 2025 paper extracts for each trait it studies. Every quantity in this theme is the same operation performed on that direction -- a dot product between an activation and the vector -- read at a different point in the model's life, starting at the earliest point of all: before the model has said anything.

  2. Projection-based monitoring of prompt-induced persona shifts

    Projection-based monitoring of prompt-induced persona shifts takes that dot product at the final token of a prompt, before generation begins, and finds it correlates strongly (r=0.75-0.83) with the trait expression score of the response that follows. The paper validates this deployment-time monitor against two methods for pushing a model toward or away from a trait by construction, starting with the simpler of the two.

  3. System-prompting

    System-prompting, a custom instruction prepended to the model's context, is the first of those two elicitation methods: a direct, explicit lever for making a trait more or less likely to appear in the response. The second method reaches the same result more indirectly, through examples rather than instruction.

  4. Many-shot prompting (as a persona-elicitation method)

    Many-shot prompting, following Anil et al. (2024), prepends 0, 5, 10, 15, or 20 trait-demonstrating exemplars instead of an instruction, and validates the same monitor from a different angle: projection at the prompt's end still predicts what comes next. Together the two methods confirm the projection reads intent before it becomes text -- the same reading trick then gets pointed at training instead of prompting.

  5. Finetuning shift (activation-shift metric)

    Finetuning shift is that training-time version: it projects the change in a model's average activation, post-finetuning minus pre-finetuning, onto the persona direction, and this shift predicts post-finetuning trait expression even more strongly (r=0.76-0.97) than prompt-based projection predicts response-level expression. The same logic can be pushed one step earlier still, before any finetuning has happened at all.

  6. Projection difference (pre-finetuning data-screening metric)

    Projection difference is that earlier point: it compares a training dataset's own response projections against the base model's natural projections on the same prompts, flagging risk before a single gradient step is taken. Across all three readings -- prompt, post-hoc training shift, and pre-training dataset audit -- the steering vector the corpus knows as a tool for injecting a behavior doubles here as a sensor for one the model has not yet, or has already, undergone.
