The public NLP benchmark suite used to catch regressions

To find out what alignment training costs, you need instruments that were never built to measure alignment at all. This theme covers the standard academic NLP benchmarks, reading comprehension, entailment, coreference, and the rest, that InstructGPT (2022) runs its models against purely to catch anything RLHF might have quietly broken.

A drop on any of these relative to plain GPT-3 is exactly the evidence the paper needs for what it calls the alignment tax, the thread tying this whole group together.

To measure the alignment tax RLHF imposes on general language ability, InstructGPT runs its models against a battery of standard academic understanding benchmarks it did not design. DROP tests discrete reasoning such as counting and sorting over paragraphs; SQuAD v2 adds unanswerable questions to reading comprehension; QuAC evaluates conversational question answering; HellaSwag requires selecting the most plausible continuation of a scenario; SST is a binary sentiment classifier; and RTE and WSC, both drawn from the SuperGLUE suite for general-purpose language understanding, test textual entailment and pronoun coreference resolution respectively. None of these benchmarks were built with instruction-following or human preference in mind; they exist to test comprehension and inference competence in the abstract. Their role in this corpus is specifically evaluative: a drop in performance on any of them, relative to plain GPT-3, is evidence that RLHF fine-tuning traded away some general capability for its gains in instruction-following, which is exactly the tradeoff the alignment tax names.

Members — a guided walk

  1. DROP (Discrete Reasoning Over the content of Paragraphs)

    DROP opens the language-understanding suite as a test of discrete reasoning over paragraphs, counting and sorting facts rather than simply retrieving them, and it is one of two benchmarks the paper elevates from passive evaluation into an active tuning target.

  2. SQuAD v2

    SQuAD v2 forms the regression testbed together with DROP: both were pulled out of the eight benchmarks to actually judge the pretraining-coefficient and KL-coefficient sweeps during training, so these two shape RLHF's tradeoffs rather than merely observing them afterward.

  3. HellaSwag

    HellaSwag, which asks a model to pick the most plausible continuation of a scenario, appeared alongside DROP on the paper's original list of RLHF regressions, but the two diverge under the fix: PPO-ptx recovers enough to surpass GPT-3 on HellaSwag while still lagging on DROP, showing the alignment tax is uneven.

  4. QuAC (Question Answering in Context)

    QuAC extends the comprehension battery into dialogue, scoring conversational question answering rather than the single-turn extraction that DROP, SQuAD v2, and HellaSwag test, broadening what this suite of benchmarks counts as language understanding.

  5. SST (Stanford Sentiment Treebank)

    SST narrows back down to the simplest probe in the suite, binary sentiment classification, rounding out the battery with a task that carries almost none of the multi-step reasoning DROP demands or the plausibility judgment HellaSwag requires.

  6. SuperGLUE

    SuperGLUE is not itself one more task to score but the umbrella suite that supplies the theme's final two benchmarks, RTE and WSC, both drawn in for general-purpose language understanding rather than any of InstructGPT's own instruction-following goals.

  7. RTE (Recognizing Textual Entailment)

    RTE tests textual entailment, one of SuperGLUE's core tasks, and is the more conventional of the suite's final pair: a binary judgment scored in essentially the same form SuperGLUE originally specifies for it.

  8. WSC (Winograd Schema Challenge)

    WSC closes the theme on a methodological wrinkle: though credited to SuperGLUE, the task InstructGPT actually runs departs from SuperGLUE's canonical binary-judgment format, asking the model to generate the pronoun's referent outright, a reminder that even off-the-shelf benchmarks get reshaped in practice.
