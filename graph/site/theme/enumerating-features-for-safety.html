Feature enumeration as a safety audit

A list of everything a model could do wrong is a strange kind of safety guarantee, but it's the one this paper closes on: if every feature inside a model were catalogued and understood, dangerous behavior would have nowhere left to hide unnoticed.

This theme names that ambition and the two pieces offered toward it, a scalable way of enumerating features instead of guessing at them, and a method for tracing how those features causally depend on each other, read against a corpus that has mostly audited models by judging their outputs rather than inspecting their insides.

Cunningham et al. (2023) close by naming the ambition their method serves: enumerative safety, Elhage et al.'s (2022) proposal that a complete, human-understandable list of a model's features could guarantee it will not perform dangerous behaviors such as deception. Sparse autoencoders are offered as the tool for building that list -- a scalable, unsupervised way of enumerating features rather than guessing at them -- and feature-circuit-detection as a way of tracing how listed features causally depend on each other across layers, a step toward the paper's stated goal of an end-to-end account of how a model computes its outputs. Read against the rest of the corpus, this reframes the oversight problem: earlier papers audit a model by judging what it outputs -- reward modeling, red-teaming, revised responses -- while enumerative safety proposes auditing it by listing and inspecting its internal parts instead, with mechanistic interpretability named as the research agenda this project frames itself as advancing.

Members — a guided walk

  1. Mechanistic interpretability

    Mechanistic interpretability is the research agenda Cunningham et al. (2023) name in their introduction as the field their method aims to advance: reverse-engineering a neural network's internal computations into human-understandable parts, in service of verification, targeted editing, and mitigating risks such as deceptive behavior. The paper offers its sparse-autoencoder method as 'a foundation for future mechanistic interpretability work,' then closes with a more specific ambition inside that agenda: enumerative safety.

  2. Enumerative safety

    Enumerative safety is Elhage et al.'s (2022) proposal that a complete, human-understandable enumeration of a model's features could itself guarantee the model will not perform dangerous behaviors such as deception -- an audit conducted by listing and inspecting a model's internal parts, rather than by judging its outputs. Cunningham et al. (2023) name it explicitly in their conclusion as the long-term motivation their method is a step toward, careful to describe their own contribution as a step, not an achievement of the goal itself. Sparse autoencoders are the specific tool the paper offers for building such a list.

  3. Sparse autoencoders (SAEs)

    Sparse autoencoders give enumerative safety a scalable, unsupervised way of enumerating features rather than guessing at them by hand: the same architecture used throughout the paper for interpretability and causal-precision results is, in the conclusion, reframed as a method for producing the complete feature inventory enumerative safety would require. A list of features alone is not a full account of how a model computes, though -- the paper's remaining contribution addresses how listed features relate to one another.

  4. Automatic feature circuit detection

    Automatic feature circuit detection extends the enumeration from a flat list into a structure: given a target feature, it ablates each upstream feature in the previous layer and ranks them by how much their removal lowers the target's activation, tracing causal dependencies across layers. Cunningham et al. (2023) name this as a step toward an end-to-end account of how a model computes its outputs, the goal beyond enumeration alone that their conclusion sets up. Read against the rest of the corpus, this reframes the oversight problem: where earlier work audits a model by judging what it outputs, enumerative safety proposes auditing it by listing and tracing its internal parts instead.
