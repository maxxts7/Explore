A behavior becomes a direction in activation space

Ask where a behavior like refusal or sycophancy actually lives inside a language model, and the answer this theme gives is oddly literal: it lives along one direction in the model's activation space, and pushing the model's internal state along that direction, by a chosen amount and in a chosen sign, moves the behavior up or down like a dial. Getting from a vague tendency to a genuine, tunable direction is the whole trick, and it rests on a small piece of arithmetic rather than any retraining.

The page walks through how that direction gets built: averaging the activation difference between many closely matched pairs of answers, then adding the result back into the model at every position after the prompt, scaled by a signed multiplier. It also covers the guardrails around that core move, from normalizing vectors so different behaviors can be compared fairly, to the refinements the authors propose for narrowing where in the network the intervention lands.

A steering vector is a single direction in a model's activation space that, added to a forward pass, shifts output toward or away from a high-level behavior. Contrastive Activation Addition is the 2023 paper's method for building and applying one: the vector is added at every token position after the user's prompt, with a signed coefficient giving continuous control. CAA steering vector construction is the specific recipe, formalized as Equation 1: extract layer-L residual stream activations at the answer-letter token position for both completions in a dataset of paired prompts, then average their difference across the whole dataset. That averaging step applies the Mean Difference method, a pre-existing extraction technique the paper notes was also used by Zou et al. and Li et al. The multiple-choice contrast-pair format keeps the pairs clean: each pair is a question ending in answer letter A or B, so positive and negative prompts differ by exactly one token, validated by an appendix experiment showing the model justifies whichever letter it is conditioned on having chosen. The steering multiplier then doses the vector: sign selects direction, magnitude selects strength, though large magnitudes degrade text quality. Steering vector normalization standardizes magnitude across behaviors while deliberately leaving the residual stream's own layer-to-layer norm growth, the accumulating sum the vector is injected into, unnormalized. Targeted-token steering and steering outside the residual stream are the paper's own proposed refinements: narrowing the intervention to fewer token positions, or moving it to another point in the network, such as after the MLP, to better localize where behavior lives. Persona vectors are the 2025 paper's successor object: also a single direction in a model's residual-stream activation space, but keyed to a personality or character trait, such as evil, sycophancy, or hallucination, rather than to one of CAA's seven hand-picked behaviors, and computed the same way in spirit, as a difference in mean activations between trait-exhibiting and non-exhibiting responses. What makes it a successor rather than a rename is that it comes with its own automated, trait-agnostic extraction pipeline, so a vector for a newly named trait no longer needs a hand-built contrast dataset. Response-conditioned contrastive vector computation is that pipeline's construction recipe, playing the role CAA steering vector construction plays for CAA: for each extraction-set question it generates ten rollouts under a positive and a negative system prompt, filters them by trait expression score to keep only responses that clearly match their intended prompt, extracts residual-stream activations averaged over the response tokens rather than a single answer-letter token, and takes the mean-difference between trait-exhibiting and non-exhibiting activations at each layer, selecting whichever layer's vector steers most effectively. It reuses the same Mean Difference averaging step CAA's recipe applies, but over full free-form rollouts screened by an LLM judge instead of over matched multiple-choice pairs differing by one token.

Members — a guided walk

  1. Steering vector

    A steering vector is a single direction in a language model's activation space that encodes a high-level behavior: adding it to the model's activations during a forward pass shifts output toward that behavior, and subtracting it shifts output away. Prior work built such vectors from single prompt pairs, from optimization, or from linear probes. The steering vector is the object every activation-engineering technique in this family produces, and the one Contrastive Activation Addition constructs by averaging over a dataset of contrast pairs rather than a single pair.

  2. Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA)

    Contrastive Activation Addition, the 2023 paper's own method, is how such a vector gets built and used. During inference the vector is added to the residual stream at every token position after the user's prompt, with a coefficient that can be positive or negative, giving continuous control over how strongly the targeted behavior appears. CAA is evaluated on Llama 2 Chat across seven alignment-relevant behaviors, tested with both multiple-choice questions and open-ended generation.

  3. CAA steering vector construction (Mean Difference over multiple-choice contrast pairs)

    The construction procedure behind that vector is formalized as Equation 1. For a dataset of (prompt, positive completion, negative completion) triples, the paper extracts layer-L residual stream activations at the token position of the answer letter for both the positive and negative completion, then averages the difference between them across the whole dataset. That single averaged difference vector is what gets added back into the residual stream at inference time.

  4. Mean Difference (MD) vector extraction

    The averaging step that construction performs is itself a named, pre-existing technique: Mean Difference vector extraction, which produces a direction by averaging the activation difference between two sets of contrasting examples. The paper notes Mean Difference has previously been used by Li et al. (2023) to find a truthfulness direction and by Zou et al. (2023) as one of several extraction methods for representation engineering, and that it produces results similar to PCA-based extraction.

  5. Multiple-choice contrast-pair format

    What makes CAA's averaging effective is the format of the pairs being averaged. Each contrast pair is a multiple-choice question ending in answer letter A or B, so the positive and negative prompts differ by exactly one token, isolating the behavior while canceling out other variation. An appendix experiment validates the design: conditioned on having already answered A or B, the model naturally continues by justifying whichever behavior that letter corresponds to, confirming the format actually elicits the target behavior.

  6. Steering multiplier

    Once a vector is built, the steering multiplier controls how it is applied: a scalar coefficient multiplying the vector before it is added into the residual stream. Its sign selects direction -- amplifying or suppressing the behavior -- and its magnitude sets the dose. Large magnitudes degrade the quality of generated text, so the paper finds a usable range by sweeping multipliers at whichever layer proves optimal for steering.

  7. Steering vector normalization choices

    Choosing a multiplier requires vectors whose magnitudes are comparable in the first place, which is where normalization enters as a design decision. The paper normalizes steering vector magnitude to be comparable across the seven behaviors, but deliberately does not normalize across layers, preserving the residual stream's own natural growth in norm over the forward pass rather than flattening it out.

  8. Residual stream

    That natural growth belongs to the residual stream itself: the running sum of layer outputs that a transformer's activations flow through and accumulate in as they pass through the network. It is the site of both operations already described -- CAA extracts its steering vectors from this stream at a chosen layer, and injects them back into it at inference, at every token position after the prompt.

  9. Steering at targeted token positions (proposed extension)

    With the method's mechanics established, the paper turns to what it might change. One proposed refinement addresses where within a generation the vector applies: rather than every token position after the prompt, targeted-token steering would intervene at a smaller, more targeted subset of positions, aiming for a better trade-off between how much the representation is perturbed and how much text quality degrades as a result.

  10. Steering outside the residual stream (proposed extension)

    A second proposed refinement addresses where within the network the vector applies. Rather than the residual stream, CAA-style intervention could target another point, such as after the MLP but before it merges back into the stream. Testing other injection points, the paper suggests, could help localize where in the model a behavior's representation actually lives, rather than just where it is convenient to read and write it.

  11. Persona vector

    Persona vectors are the 2025 paper's successor object: also a single direction in a model's residual-stream activation space, but keyed to a personality or character trait such as evil, sycophancy, or hallucination, rather than one of CAA's seven hand-picked behaviors, and computed the same way in spirit, as a difference in mean activations between trait-exhibiting and non-exhibiting responses. What makes it a successor rather than a rename is its own automated, trait-agnostic extraction pipeline, covered next.

  12. Response-conditioned contrastive vector computation

    Response-conditioned contrastive vector computation is that pipeline's construction recipe, playing the role CAA steering vector construction plays for CAA: for each question it generates rollouts under positive and negative system prompts, filters them by trait expression score, extracts residual-stream activations averaged over response tokens, and takes the mean-difference between trait-exhibiting and non-exhibiting activations at each layer, keeping whichever layer steers most effectively. It closes the theme by reusing CAA's own Mean Difference averaging step, now over free-form rollouts instead of matched multiple-choice pairs.
