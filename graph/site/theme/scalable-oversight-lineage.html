From an open problem to reward models to AI feedback

Scalable oversight is this corpus's clearest throughline: a problem Concrete Problems (2016) could only name, InstructGPT (2022) turns into a working reward model, and Constitutional AI (2022) then scales again by replacing the human labeler with an AI one. This theme follows that lineage across all three papers.

Told as one continuous story rather than three separate ones, it slows down at RL from AI Feedback, the point where an AI's judgment starts doing the job a human used to do.

This theme traces the corpus's most important cross-paper throughline. Concrete Problems names scalable oversight as an open problem in 2016: safe behavior requires evaluating a true objective too costly to check on every example. InstructGPT's reward model is, functionally, a scaled solution, a learned model trained to predict which of two outputs human labelers would prefer, letting reinforcement learning from human feedback optimize a cheap, dense proxy for a genuinely expensive human judgment across an entire training distribution. Constitutional AI's RL from AI Feedback pushes the same lineage one step further: the feedback model that produces harmlessness preference labels replaces even the human labeler, and the hybrid human/AI preference model blends human-labeled helpfulness comparisons with AI-labeled harmlessness ones into a single reward signal. Iterated online training, periodically refreshing the preference model on the current policy's own outputs, is flagged as a further scaling step now made easier once an AI, rather than a human, is doing the labeling. Read together, these concepts show one problem named once, operationalized once, and then scaled again.

Members — a guided walk

  1. Scalable oversight

    Scalable oversight is named as an open problem in 2016: safe behavior requires evaluating a true objective too costly to check on every example. This walk traces how that named-but-unsolved problem was, in hindsight, operationalized and then scaled again across two later papers.

  2. Reward model (RM)

    The reward model is, functionally, a scaled solution to scalable oversight: a learned model trained to predict which of two outputs human labelers would prefer, letting reinforcement learning optimize a cheap, dense proxy for an expensive human judgment across an entire training distribution. InstructGPT itself never uses the term scalable oversight, so this connection is visible only in hindsight.

  3. Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF)

    RLHF is the fine-tuning technique the reward model exists to serve: its architecture and role are dictated by how policy optimization actually works, since it starts from the same weights as the policy it will later steer and anchors the reward signal RLHF optimizes against. Here the lineage's second link, an algorithm built entirely around a learned preference model, is complete.

  4. RL from AI Feedback (RLAIF)

    RLAIF swaps the labeler in RLHF, replacing the human who produces preference comparisons with an AI system, while Constitutional AI states plainly that the remainder of the pipeline, preference model training and RL, is exactly the same as RLHF. It is a minimal, surgical modification, not a new algorithm, pushing the same lineage one step further toward automation.

  5. Feedback Model

    The feedback model is what RLAIF delegates harmlessness comparisons to: an independent language model shown a harmful prompt and a pair of responses, formatted as an explicit multiple-choice question, that produces the preference label a human labeler would have produced before. It is the concrete mechanism that makes RLAIF's labeler-swap actually work.

  6. Hybrid Human/AI Preference Model

    The hybrid human/AI preference model is trained on a mixture the feedback model makes possible: human-labeled helpfulness comparisons stitched together with AI-labeled harmlessness comparisons into a single reward signal. It shows the lineage does not have to be all-human or all-AI, human and machine judgment can be blended in the same trained model.

  7. Iterated (Online) Training

    Iterated online training, periodically refreshing the preference model with new comparisons on the current policy's own outputs, already existed as a valuable technique using human feedback, but RLAIF makes it newly automatable now that an AI rather than a human is doing the labeling. It closes the lineage by pointing at the next place scaling pressure will fall: keeping the preference model itself on-distribution as the policy keeps changing.
